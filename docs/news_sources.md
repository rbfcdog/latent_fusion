# News sources for the fusion experiment

## Evaluation period

Use a contiguous period with a reliable news feed, not only trading days with articles. Selecting only news days biases evaluation toward event-heavy periods. Within a covered period, retain both news and no-news days and report their results separately.

None of the 49 local raw news files contains January 2024 articles. Treat this as a feed gap, not evidence that no news occurred. Exclude the gap from the primary text evaluation. If it cannot be repaired, report a contiguous pre-gap test as a limited result, not evidence of performance in 2024–25. Preserve chronological train/validation/test splits and purge forward-label windows at split boundaries.

## Candidate sources

| Source | Use | Limitation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [GDELT](https://www.gdeltproject.org/data.html) | Free historical article discovery and archive searches | Company matching needs verification; indexed records do not guarantee usable full article text. |
| [Massive stock news](https://massive.com/docs/rest/stocks/news) | Ticker-linked historical titles, descriptions, and publication timestamps | Its documentation lists two years of history on Stocks Basic and all history on Stocks Starter; verify access and article-text licensing before committing. |
| [SEC EDGAR](https://www.sec.gov/edgar/sec-api-documentation) | Historical filings and company-event text | Filings are not a substitute for daily news; evaluate them as a separate source. |

First sample January 2024 for a few tickers. Check publication timestamps, ticker/entity relevance, duplicate URLs, and trading-day coverage. If a source works, backfill the entire study period consistently rather than patching January alone: a provider change can otherwise look like a change in the text signal. Keep unknown feed coverage distinct from genuinely quiet news days; lag text when same-day availability is uncertain.

## Audited event dataset for the Colab signal test

The 49 local ticker files contain 124,596 tagged records dated January 2020–November 2023. Some `Article` bodies do not match their own `Article_title` and URL, so **do not embed the bodies**. `scripts/export_event_signal.py` retains 27,654 distinct headlines only after verifying that title and URL agree and that the headline identifies the tagged issuer or fund. This is a high-precision subset, **not** a complete news archive; no event in the export does not establish that no news existed.

The exporter writes gitignored `data/fusion_events.jsonl.gz` (one timestamped, source-attributed headline per event) and `data/fusion_event_panel.npz` (cached MOMENT plus a five-trading-day excess-return target). It maps publication to the first trading day on or after its date and delays availability by one additional trading day. It limits the experiment to **2020-01-01 through 2023-11-30** rather than treating the 2024 feed failure as quiet news. The available GDELT DOC request for January 2024 returned HTTP 429; there is no configured historical paid-news API key. Backfill remains unresolved.

The Colab notebook `notebooks/05_fusion/event_signal_colab.ipynb` uses these two exported files, trains only on Colab GPU, and compares an event-gated residual against the same-date price-only portfolio and equal-weight buy-and-hold after costs. The ticker universe was selected retrospectively by news coverage and includes ETFs: selection bias and event relevance remain research limitations.

The Colab panel loader preserves `datetime64[ns]` trading dates. Portfolio calendar checks must compare prediction and price dates at the same resolution; truncating one side to day precision falsely reports missing sessions.

## Text-first attention experiment (Colab)

Run `notebooks/05_fusion/text_attention_colab.ipynb` on a Colab GPU using the same audited `fusion_events.jsonl.gz` and `fusion_event_panel.npz` already in `MyDrive/latent_fusion_data/`. It reuses the cached MiniLM title vectors and writes **new** reports/checkpoints to `MyDrive/latent_fusion_data/text_attention_results/`; no local fusion run is required.

Each title contributes its frozen embedding, 17 bounded, interpretable cues (event class, explicit earnings/guidance/rating language, observed percentage, update/rumor/uncertainty), age and novelty against up to ten earlier-available titles for the same ticker. These cues describe the headline, **not necessarily the tagged company's role**; percentages are not analyst-consensus surprises. Same-day headlines do not establish novelty for one another. Numeric history queries self-attended title events, but cannot reach the prediction head without a recent event. A mask/age-only attention control tests whether the title content adds information beyond event timing; price-only, zero-excess prediction and buy-and-hold are separate baselines.

In a data-only check of all 27,654 audited titles, 22,821 fell outside the five explicit event classes and 3,211 matched earnings/guidance. This sparse taxonomy is an extra cue, not a complete language representation; the embedding remains available to attention. Train/validation/test follow the existing temporal, purged panel split. The 2022–23 test was examined in the previous experiment, so new results on it remain exploratory; an independently gathered post-gap news period is necessary before a confirmatory performance claim.

## LLM headline extraction and attention ablation (Colab)

Run `notebooks/05_fusion/llm_attention_colab.ipynb` on a Colab T4/L4 GPU with the **same** two audited Drive artifacts. It downloads the pinned, [Apache-2.0 Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct checkpoint](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct) in 4-bit mode, so the first run needs substantial time and disk space. No external LLM API key or local inference is required. The per-title JSONL cache in `MyDrive/latent_fusion_data/llm_headline_cache/` resumes after Colab disconnects; rerun the extraction cell until the whole manifest is complete, then run the training cell. Outputs go to a separate `llm_attention_results/` folder.

The LLM sees only each verified title plus the tagged ticker and its known aliases. It returns issuer role, event class, explicit direction and certainty, and a literal percentage with an exact title quotation. Unknown/ambiguous roles abstain; malformed or unsupported responses are saved with their raw output and `status=invalid`, not passed off as facts. The notebook displays an extraction audit and refuses to train when more than 5% of titles have invalid responses. A valid `unknown` is distinct from an invalid response.

The experiment holds the numeric-query attention architecture, title dates, novelty, and event masks fixed while comparing frozen MiniLM **with and without** these LLM facts; it also reports price-only, zero-excess and buy-and-hold controls on the same test period. A better classification of headline wording does **not** establish a better forecast. The Qwen checkpoint postdates the 2020–23 headlines and may encode later outcomes despite title-only prompting; the already-inspected 2022–23 test is exploratory. A new, consistently covered period is needed for confirmation.

## Direct contextual LLM forecast without text/price embeddings (Colab)

`notebooks/05_fusion/llm_context_only_colab.ipynb` calls `src/fusion/llm_context_experiment.py` with the same audited panel and title manifest, but reads **only known returns, past volatility, dates, and observed labels for later evaluation**; cached MOMENT vectors and MiniLM embeddings never enter the prompt or predictor. Each of the eight assets receives the last 20 known daily returns, same-close returns for the eight-asset basket, and up to eight available headlines from its previous five trading sessions. A single LLM directly predicts five-session excess return and quotes exact supplied titles as evidence; there is no downstream learned head. Model responses and input hashes are cached per model for restart, and malformed or fabricated quotations abort before evaluation.

Configure `LLM_CONTEXT_BACKEND=api` plus the provider's actual `LLM_CONTEXT_MODEL_ID` for an OpenAI-compatible chat-completions endpoint (`LLM_CONTEXT_BASE_URL` if needed, API key entered privately), or `LLM_CONTEXT_BACKEND=transformers` plus an instruct model ID for 4-bit inference on a remote GPU. Provider compatibility and the commercial model's actual API identifier are **not verified**. Separate model folders hold predictions, signal metrics, 10-bps long-only portfolio results and a buy-and-hold chart; the saved numeric-only forecast is checked for matching labels, coverage and dates as a comparison, not passed to the LLM. The 2022–23 evaluation is already inspected, and a post-2023 model may retain historical outcomes in its weights: no new-period claim is justified before complete, licensed 2024–25 news coverage exists.

## Stateful LLM buy/sell agent (Colab)

`notebooks/05_fusion/llm_trading_agent_colab.ipynb` runs `src/fusion/llm_trading_agent.py` on the same verified eight-equity test. Every five common sessions, one remote LLM call sees trailing known prices, available headlines and its own current holdings, then returns `BUY` (enter), `SELL` (exit) or `HOLD` for each asset with source-title evidence. Empty holdings stay 100% in cash; held assets get equal long-only target weights. Trades incur 10 bps per absolute traded asset weight, and begin earning returns only after the decision close. Interruption resumes a model-specific audited JSONL cache with recalculated positions and prompt hashes. Invalid actions, invented citations or misaligned saved numeric forecasts abort.

The notebook first computes the **non-LLM** numeric-agent reference from already saved eight-asset numeric predictions: enter on positive five-session excess forecasts, exit on negative ones, maintain otherwise. In the already inspected 2022–23 test, this particular equal-weight action rule returned **28.25956% net** against the same-calendar eight-asset buy-and-hold **20.72928% net**, while all-cash returned **0%** (10-bps trading costs). This is an exploratory numeric comparator, **not** independently validated alpha.

An authenticated `gpt-6-luna` API run was completed from the local machine on the existing historical test, without local model execution: **58** basket decisions from 2022-09-29 through 2023-11-16 (portfolio returns through 2023-11-24), **28 BUY, 24 SELL, 412 HOLD** actions. The LLM agent returned **16.27840% net**, **4.45088 percentage points below buy-and-hold** and **11.98116 points below the numeric-action reference**. Its maximum observed drawdown was **−18.238%**, compared with **−14.657%** for buy-and-hold. Request/response evidence and results are saved under the gitignored `data/llm_trading_agent_results/api-gpt-6-luna/`. The hosted model rejects `temperature=0`, so the API call uses its default sampling temperature and persists each accepted answer for reproducibility; reruns with a fresh cache can differ. These returns are positive in absolute terms but provide **no evidence of text-driven alpha** on an already inspected, possibly memorized historical period. New-period validation remains blocked by unverified news coverage.

The offline cost audit replays the **same saved actions**, not new LLM inference. At 0 bps / 10 bps, Luna returned **18.82910% / 16.27840%** (2.55070 percentage points of compounded trading-cost drag, total traded weight 21.69226); the numeric-only action policy returned **38.04633% / 28.25956%** (9.78677 points of drag, traded weight 73.48219). Buy-and-hold returned **20.85013% / 20.72928%**. API expenses in USD are not part of these percentage returns; no starting capital was specified.

The original 58 Luna responses saved only text and actions, **not API `usage`**, so their actual token counts and USD bill cannot be reconstructed from that cache. `src/fusion/llm_costs.py` writes `trading_cost_comparison.csv`, `api_usage_ledger.csv`, and `api_cost_summary.json`; the old calls are labeled **unpriced**, not free. From now on, every returned API response is appended to an attempts log with provider model, response ID, input/output, cached and reasoning token details **before** JSON validation, including unsuccessful-format responses. An API error with no usage response cannot be priced. New GPT-6 Luna responses with sufficient usage and the Standard tier receive an estimated **list-price** quote based on [official rates](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/pricing), checked on 2026-10-21: per million short-context tokens **$0.10 input, $0.50 output, $0.01 cached input**. Nonstandard tiers, unknown cache accounting or missing usage remain unpriced. A separate 11-input/15-output-token diagnostic call confirmed the API supplies usage and had an estimated list-price cost of **$0.0000086**, **not** the cost of any of the 58 trading decisions. Invoicing, discounts, retries without a response, and USD-to-return conversion require the provider's billing records and a capital amount.

## Interseção fixa Luna × numérico (execução local)

`src/fusion/llm_numeric_hybrid.py` reconstitui as posições independentes do Luna e da política numérica em cada uma das **58 datas** já auditadas. Só mantém uma ação quando **ambos** mantêm o ativo após a decisão; aplica pesos iguais aos ativos em consenso e fica em caixa se nenhum coincidir. Uma entrada pode ocorrer quando o numérico passa a comprar algo que o Luna já possuía; qualquer saída de uma das políticas desfaz a posição. ## Fast fusion presentation demo (Colab)

`notebooks/05_fusion/fast_fusion_demo_colab.ipynb` replaces the slow 7B LLM extraction with a small, genuine multimodal attention comparison. It uses the **existing Drive panel for dates and observed returns only**, not its stored MOMENT vectors. On each Colab GPU run it generates fresh MOMENT-1-small embeddings from the previous 64 known daily returns for every required numeric input and fresh MiniLM embeddings for **all 8,455 audited titles** of eight fixed assets (ADBE, BABA, COST, CVX, GILD, NKE, PYPL, QCOM). The verified titles already have the conservative one-session delay; days without titles remain in the evaluation. No separate price CSV upload or old article-embedding cache is required.

On Python 3.13, `momentfm==0.1.4` cannot resolve its pinned NumPy 1.25.2 and Transformers 4.33.3 dependencies. The notebook installs MOMENT without those dependency pins, keeps a supported Transformers 4.x release, and checks the MOMENT import before extraction. This version combination is not upstream-supported; verify the actual GPU run in Colab before presenting its results.

The notebook trains at most eight epochs on the chronological, purged panel and compares numeric-only against numeric-query cross-attention over headlines. It saves newly inferred embedding arrays, model identities, generation timestamp, signal/coverage metrics, long-only backtests after 10 bps, an eight-asset buy-and-hold curve, and a zero-excess **forecast** reference in `MyDrive/latent_fusion_data/fast_fusion_demo_results/`. The target is five-session forward return relative to the **same eight-asset basket** plotted as buy-and-hold, not relative to the earlier 49-asset universe.

This is a smaller **demonstration**, not a replacement for the 49-asset MOMENT-large study or proof of profitable text. The asset subset was chosen ex post for news coverage, 2022–23 test outcomes were already examined, and no 2024+ source gap is filled. Generation time depends on the Colab GPU and model downloads; it avoids the much slower 7B token-by-token decoding rather than promising a fixed runtime.

## Frozen-baseline residual attention (Colab)

`notebooks/05_fusion/fast_residual_attention_colab.ipynb` reuses `fast_demo_panel.npz`, `fast_demo_events.npz`, `ts_only.pt`, and `fast_demo_provenance.json` from the completed fast demo. It checks that the saved checkpoint and embeddings belong to the same run; no MOMENT, MiniLM, or LLM inference is repeated. A zero-initialized, bounded attention residual trains over a frozen numeric predictor, separately with headline age/count only and with the actual headline features. No recent headline means the unchanged numeric prediction. Only training/validation choose the checkpoint; all variants use the same full test calendar, long-only backtest, ten-basis-point costs and eight-asset buy-and-hold. Outputs are written separately to `MyDrive/latent_fusion_data/fast_residual_attention_results/`. A gain over the age/count control is needed to argue for textual content; the previously inspected 2022–23 test is exploratory and does not confirm future performance.

It displays chronological split/news coverage, the architecture and its metadata ablation, validation histories, forecast error and cross-sectional IC, long-only net-of-cost portfolio results, an equity curve, and the actual magnitude of prediction changes. The presentation notebook also addresses the TODO's investor-profile and worst-case/cost analyses *at the allocation layer only*: three fixed, fully invested long-only basket policies (63/21/5 trading-session rebalance intervals, 18%/25%/40% per-asset caps, progressively stronger score tilts), first/last weights, same-calendar numeric/metadata/text/BH comparison, drawdown, turnover, 0/10/25-bps transaction-cost sensitivity, worst observed month, per-ticker and news-availability forecast error. The notebook now adds two **Colab GPU-only** falsification baselines: a residual trained on headline content shuffled among already-available articles while preserving article dates/ages/masks, and a content-only model using MiniLM, lexical/sentiment cues and novelty but **no numeric/price inputs or article age**. Outputs are stored separately in `MyDrive/latent_fusion_data/attention_presentation_controls/`. Without a GPU or matching saved control results, the notebook says the controls are pending and reports no invented scores. These 2022–23 comparisons are exploratory; a shuffled-content result is a negative control, not a new holdout.

It reports regime-wise price-only, metadata, text and (if calculated) new-control MSE/IC and headline coverage, with small-regime warnings. A state at close $t$ is unavailable before that close, never changes portfolio weights in this notebook, and is not a claim that regime switching improves returns. The saved-panel smoke check found 2,320 test rows across 290 dates, of which 144 rows are high-volatility; results have overlapping five-day targets.

## New-period price handoff and the news blocker

`src/fusion/new_period_news.py` exports the **311 observed common trading sessions** from 2024-01-02 through 2025-03-28 for ADBE, BABA, COST, CVX, GILD, NKE, PYPL and QCOM from local `data/time_series/*.csv`. `build_new_period_package(out_dir, price_dir=...)` writes `prices.csv`, `new_period_prices.npz` (8 × 311 closes and known trailing returns) and `coverage.json` (source-file SHA-256 hashes). Existing source files resemble Yahoo-format prices; their historical corporate-action adjustment is **not independently certified**. No forward targets, fresh MOMENT/MiniLM vectors or new-period strategy scores are generated by this handoff. Both local and Drive results mark the news feed `missing_or_unverified` and `ready=false`; an absent feed is never counted as 311 quiet news days.

GDELT January 2024 requests returned HTTP 429 and Massive historical news returned HTTP 401 without API credentials. Historical Google News RSS returned suspect publication dates and its copyright forbids this use. A separately licensed, genuinely complete ticker-linked 2024–25 title feed is still required. For an operator-supplied feed, place eight `TICKER.jsonl` files plus `coverage.json` (named provider, complete requested window, HTTPS retrieval URL and retrieval time) in `MyDrive/latent_fusion_data/verified_news_2024_2025/`. Each article must include ticker, timezone-aware `published_at`, `title`, `source` and HTTPS `url`. The importer validates those fields, ticker relevance, deduplication and a conservative one-session decision delay (two sessions if published after the US close). It labels coverage **operator-attested, not independently verified**; incomplete feeds do not produce an `articles.jsonl`. Once actual new-period news is available, generate **new** MOMENT/MiniLM embeddings in Colab and evaluate a properly aligned model before making out-of-sample claims. The 2020–23 panel/checkpoint cannot be applied to 2024–25 without rebuilding features.
